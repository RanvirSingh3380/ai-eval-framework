Q7:how can we calculate AI model accuracy?
Actual: Calculating AI model accuracy involves evaluating the performance of a machine learning model by comparing its predictions with the actual outcomes. Here are the common methods to calculate AI model accuracy:

1. **Confusion Matrix**: A table used to describe the performance of a classification model. It compares the predicted values with the actual values and provides metrics such as:
	* **True Positives (TP)**: Correctly predicted positive values.
	* **True Negatives (TN)**: Correctly predicted negative values.
	* **False Positives (FP)**: Incorrectly predicted positive values.
	* **False Negatives (FN)**: Incorrectly predicted negative values.
2. **Accuracy**: The proportion of correctly predicted values out of total predictions.
	* **Accuracy = (TP + TN) / (TP + TN + FP + FN)**
3. **Precision**: The proportion of true positives out of all predicted positive values.
	* **Precision = TP / (TP + FP)**
4. **Recall**: The proportion of true positives out of all actual positive values.
	* **Recall = TP / (TP + FN)**
5. **F1 Score**: The harmonic mean of precision and recall.
	* **F1 Score = 2 \* (Precision \* Recall) / (Precision + Recall)**
6. **Mean Squared Error (MSE)**: The average of the squared differences between predicted and actual values, used for regression problems.
	* **MSE = (1/n) \* ∑(Predicted - Actual)^2**
7. **Mean Absolute Error (MAE)**: The average of the absolute differences between predicted and actual values, used for regression problems.
	* **MAE = (1/n) \* ∑|Predicted - Actual|**
8. **R-Squared (R2)**: A measure of how well a regression model fits the data, ranging from 0 to 1.
	* **R2 = 1 - (SSE / SST)**, where SSE is the sum of squared errors and SST is the total sum of squares.
9. **Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve (AUC-ROC)**: A measure of a binary classification model's ability to distinguish between positive and negative classes.
10. **K-Fold Cross-Validation**: A technique to evaluate a model's performance by splitting the data into k folds, training on k-1 folds, and testing on the remaining fold.

To calculate these metrics, you can use libraries such as:

* Scikit-learn (Python)
* TensorFlow (Python)
* PyTorch (Python)
* R ( caret package)
* MATLAB (Statistics and Machine Learning Toolbox)

Remember to split your data into training and testing sets to evaluate your model's performance on unseen data.
